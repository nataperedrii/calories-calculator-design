Brand directions · v2

Three light-theme directions for a photo-first calorie calculator that must pass App Store and Google Play review. Each board has a palette named after food, fonts, tokens, three 390×844 mini screens (Home, Photo analysis result, Food diary) and an app icon preview. All food numbers are USDA FoodData Central values per 100 g, scaled to the portion shown.

Step 0 · Direct competitors

MyFitnessPal · Yazio · Lifesum · FatSecret

Do well
  • Several items per photo, confirmed before saving (MyFitnessPal)
  • Adjust each ingredient's amount after a photo (Lifesum)
  • Recipes that fit meal targets (Yazio)
  • Verified database, free core features (FatSecret)
Do poorly
  • No recognition confidence shown
  • Photo and barcode logging behind paywalls
  • Crowdsourced duplicates, ads, AI pop-ups
  • Red "over goal" numbers, good/bad labels

MVP scope (from research.md)

What we take, and why

  • Photo analysis with confirmation and editable portions. The core feature
  • Confidence per item. Nobody shows it; fixes trust
  • Home summary and diary by meal. Found in all four
  • Search (recent foods first) and barcode. Two taps to log
  • Dish calculator with cooked weight (user story 1)
  • Recipes that fit your day (user story 2)
Out of scope
Social feedMarketplaceCoachingStreaksFastingAds

Core flow · photo analysis

Scan tab or "+ Add", then camera or library
Camera purpose screen, then the system prompt
Guidance: plate outline · light · 45° · live hints
Analyzing 2–4 s · "Finding foods…" · Cancel
Result: items · grams · kcal · P/F/C · confidence
Add to Lunch, then a toast with Undo
Errors: not recognized → Retake / Search · offline queue

Colour and food

Warm = appetite, green = fresh, oat = wholesome

Tomato / persimmonHoney / mustardHerbs / avocadoOat / rice / coconut
Clinical cyanPure #FFFFFFBlue surfacesViolet gradientsGrey UI
Macro rule
  • Protein, fat and carbs are always a magenta / gold / blue triad. ΔE ≥ 22 under protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia
  • Always labelled P · F · C with values, in a fixed order
  • Over goal = warning tone, never an error

System and store

  • 8 pt grid (4 pt fine steps) · margins 16–20 · frame 390×844
  • Touch targets ≥ 44 pt / 48 dp · body 16 · caption ≥ 12 · Dynamic Type
  • Safe areas: status bar 47, home indicator 34 · nav with 5 items
  • Icon 1024², no transparency, no pre-rounded corners · Android adaptive, 66/108 safe zone
  • Name ≤ 30 characters, no trademarks · Google Fonts (OFL) only
  • Camera purpose screen · in-app account deletion · "Not medical advice"
  • Default goal = maintenance · pace ≤ 0.5 kg per week · floor 1,200 kcal

How it's different

A low-pressure tracker: no red for going over, no streaks, and the day shown as a plate ring split by meals rather than a countdown. It's aimed at the people the research showed feel judged by red numbers and good/bad ratings.

Recommendation: A · RIPE

Strongest food story for a photo-first app. The photo is the interface. Oat and Persimmon frame food rather than competing with it, and every palette name is literally something you eat.

Answers the two biggest category problems. A camera-first tab bar deals with slow logging. A confidence label on every detected item, with alternatives and a search fallback, deals with distrust.

Passes the responsible-nutrition bar. Friendly copy, over goal shown as a warning tone (never an error), a maintenance default and a clear "Not medical advice".

Borrow from the others: PER100's label-panel rigour for the food-detail and nutrition tables, and SPRIG's neutral "over goal" language.
